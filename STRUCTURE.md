Structural Matrix — Formal Role & Transition Architecture
This document defines the structural roles, transitions, and dependencies that form the core of the Structural Matrix.
Where README.md explains what the method is, and METHOD.md explains how it works, this file defines the internal architecture the method detects.

1. Overview of Structural Roles
The Structural Matrix identifies roles based purely on behaviour, not meaning.
Each role emerges from frequency patterns, transitions, and positional stability.

The primary roles are:

1. Anchor
Appears consistently at the start of cycles

Establishes the system’s “reset point”

Low entropy, high positional stability

2. Frame
Defines boundaries or structural edges

Often surrounds or contains content blocks

Provides rhythm and segmentation

3. Transition
Bridges one structural state to another

Appears between anchors and content

Medium entropy, directional behaviour

4. Content Block
Dense clusters of repeated or high‑frequency values

Represents the “payload” of the system

High entropy, low positional stability

5. Terminator
Consistently ends sequences

Marks closure or structural completion

Often low frequency but high reliability

2. Role Detection Criteria
Roles are not assigned by meaning — they are inferred from behaviour.

Anchor Criteria
Appears early in sequences

Repeats at consistent intervals

Rarely appears mid‑sequence

Low transition diversity

Frame Criteria
Appears at boundaries

Often pairs with itself (e.g., A … A)

Creates segmentation

Transition Criteria
Frequently appears between two other roles

High directional consistency

Predictable movement (e.g., Anchor → Transition → Content)

Content Block Criteria
Forms clusters

High repetition

High entropy

Often follows transitions

Terminator Criteria
Appears at the end of sequences

Rarely appears elsewhere

Low entropy, high positional reliability

3. Transition Matrix
The Structural Matrix expresses the system as a set of allowed and forbidden transitions.

Allowed Transitions (typical)
From	To	Behaviour
Anchor	Transition	System initiation
Transition	Content Block	Payload entry
Content Block	Content Block	Cluster continuation
Content Block	Terminator	Structural closure
Terminator	Anchor	Cycle reset


Forbidden / Rare Transitions
From	To	Reason
Terminator → Content	Violates closure
Content → Anchor	Breaks cluster logic
Anchor → Terminator	No payload
Transition → Anchor	Reversal of direction


These patterns are not rules you impose — they are rules the system reveals.

4. Structural Dependencies
Each role has dependencies that define how it behaves within the system.

Anchor Dependencies
Requires a stable position

Requires predictable recurrence

Frame Dependencies
Requires boundary symmetry

Often depends on Anchor placement

Transition Dependencies
Requires directional consistency

Depends on both Anchor and Content

Content Block Dependencies
Requires cluster formation

Depends on Transition entry

Terminator Dependencies
Requires closure behaviour

Depends on Content Block presence

5. System-Level Architecture
When roles and transitions are mapped, the system reveals its architecture:

Natural Systems
Irregular transitions

Variable cluster sizes

Floating anchors

High entropy

Engineered Systems
Stable anchors

Predictable transitions

Consistent cluster behaviour

Medium entropy

Constructed Systems
Modular blocks

Repeated templates

Clear segmentation

Low entropy

Hoax / Random Systems
No stable roles

No consistent transitions

No structural dependencies

Entropy without pattern

6. Structural Matrix Summary
The Structural Matrix reduces any symbolic system to:

Roles (Anchor, Frame, Transition, Content, Terminator)

Transitions (allowed, forbidden, rare)

Dependencies (positional, behavioural, rhythmic)

Architecture (natural, engineered, constructed, random)

This creates a mathematical fingerprint of the system.
